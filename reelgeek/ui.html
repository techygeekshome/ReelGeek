Before you post

  • Add trending audio in the app, not here. Native sound gets better distribution than baked-in music, and it keeps you clear of copyright claims. The cuts sit on a clean tempo grid so a track at a similar BPM will lock on.
  • The first second decides everything. Lead with your strongest photo and a hook line people have to resolve.
  • Post the same edit to both platforms. The 1080x1920 file works as-is for TikTok and YouTube Shorts.
  • Re-roll before you settle. Same photos, new seed, different edit. Pick the one that feels best.
Worth saying plainly: this controls the craft, not the algorithm. A good edit gives good photos their best chance. It cannot manufacture reach on its own.
What ReelGeek will not do: it never uploads your photos, never posts on your behalf, and never sends anything anywhere. Every frame is rendered on this PC. The only time it touches the internet is when you press Check for updates.

What it does

Turns a folder of photos into a vertical edit. Cuts land on a tempo grid, the camera moves on every shot, and the transitions are the ones short-form editors actually use. Output is 1080x1920 H.264 MP4.

What it will not do

No account, no upload, no telemetry, nothing bundled. Your photos never leave this PC and neither do your renders. The only outbound request the app ever makes is the update check, and only when you ask for it.

Support

ReelGeek

Something not working

Open an issue on GitHub. Say what you did, what happened, and how many photos were in the edit. Screenshots help. Issues are read by a person, not a bot.

Render failed or the video looks wrong

Try Quick preview first: it renders in a few seconds and tells you whether the problem is the settings or the photos. Very large photos are downscaled automatically, so size is rarely the cause.

The edit is not landing on the beat

Set Tempo to the BPM of the track you plan to add, then re-roll. Auto length lets the style choose its own runtime, which is usually the best starting point.
